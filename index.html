1 Start here

What is an ITR, and do you even need to file one?

An ITR is simply a yearly statement of your income and taxes, filed with the Income Tax Department. Filing it is how you settle up: pay any shortfall, or claim back any tax that was already deducted from you.

You must usually file if…
  • Your total income is above the basic exemption limit (₹4,00,000 under the default new regime for FY 2025-26).
  • You want a refund — e.g. your employer deducted more TDS than your actual tax.
  • You have foreign assets, large investments, or need the return as income proof for a loan or visa.
  • You had business income, or want to carry forward a loss.
You can file voluntarily even if…
  • Your income is below the limit but tax was deducted (file to get it back).
  • You want a clean, verifiable income record for the future.
Good news for small earners

Under the new regime, income up to ₹12,00,000 is effectively tax-free (after the standard deduction and the 87A rebate). Many small businesses and modest earners owe nothing — but may still file to claim a refund.

2 Which ITR form?

Pick your form before you pick your papers

There are several ITR forms. Choosing the wrong one is the single most common reason a return gets rejected. Answer these five questions and we will point you to the right one.

1. Are you a resident individual of India? (Not an NRI or “resident but not ordinarily resident”.)
2. Is your total income more than ₹50,00,000?
3. Do you have income from a business or profession? (Shop, trading, freelance, consultancy, etc. — not just salary.)
4. Is it a small business you can declare under presumptive taxation? (Turnover within the scheme limit — roughly ₹2–3 crore for business, ₹50–75 lakh for professions. If unsure, choose “Not sure”.)
5. Any of these apply? Capital gains above ₹1.25 lakh, foreign assets or income, you are a company director, or you hold unlisted shares.

6 Step by step

The actual filing, one screen at a time

Do this on a laptop if you can — the portal is easier to read on a big screen. Every step below tells you what to do, where to find it, and the mistake to avoid.

1

Log in to the e-filing portal

incometax.gov.in → Login

Go to the official portal and log in with your PAN and password. First time here? Register using your PAN, and set up a password and a secure access message.

Watch out: Only use incometax.gov.in. Never file through a look-alike site that asks for your password or a fee to “submit”.
2

Link your PAN with Aadhaar

Profile → Link Aadhaar

If not already linked, do it now — an unlinked PAN can make your return invalid. Check the status in your profile; if it is pending, follow the prompt and pay the late linking fee if one applies.

Watch out: The name and date of birth must match exactly on both documents. A single mismatch is a common reason linking fails.
3

Choose the right year and form

e-File → Income Tax Returns → File ITR

Select the correct Assessment Year, then the ITR form you picked in Step 2. The portal will warn you if you choose a form that does not match your income sources.

Watch out: Do not confuse the financial year (when you earned) with the assessment year (when you file). Filing for the wrong year sends everything back.
4

Pull in the pre-filled data

Preview → Download Pre-filled / AIS

The portal can pre-fill your salary, TDS and interest from Form 16, 26AS and AIS. Download your AIS and 26AS and keep them open beside you.

Watch out: Pre-filled data is a starting point, not the final word. Reconcile every figure against your own records before you accept it.
5

Match your income to your papers

Schedule S (salary) · Schedule OS (other sources)

Cross-check your gross salary, exemptions and standard deduction against Form 16. Add interest from savings accounts and deposits — banks report this to the department, so it is visible even if you forget it.

Watch out: Undeclared savings-bank interest is one of the most common triggers for a “defective return” notice. Include even small amounts.
6

Enter deductions and taxes paid

Schedule VI-A · Schedule TDS/TCS

Claim deductions you are eligible for (more on this in Step 7). Then confirm the TDS your employer and banks already paid — it should match 26AS to the rupee.

Watch out: You can only claim deductions under the old regime. Most 80C-type deductions vanish if you pick the new regime.
7

Choose your tax regime

Regime selection screen

The new regime is the default. The portal shows your tax under both; pick whichever leaves you with less to pay. Business owners must file Form 10-IEA to opt for the old regime.

Watch out: This choice is mostly locked in for the year. Compute both options carefully before you commit.
8

Preview, validate and submit

Preview Return → Submit

Read the full preview end to end — it is your last chance to catch a typo. Fix any validation errors the portal flags, then submit. If tax is due, pay it here and note the challan number.

Watch out: Do not rush the preview. A wrong bank account number here means a delayed or failed refund.
9

E-verify — do not skip this

e-Verify → Aadhaar OTP / Net-banking / EVC

An unverified return is treated as not filed. E-verify within 30 days using an Aadhaar OTP, your net-banking, or an EVC. You will get an acknowledgement with an ITR-V reference number — save it.

Watch out: Filing without verifying is the classic silent failure. No acknowledgement number means no valid return.
You are done — now track it

After e-verification, watch your email and the portal’s “e-Proceedings” tab. When the return is processed you get an intimation under Section 143(1) confirming your refund or accepted tax. Keep it with your records.

Sunita runs a small trading business with a turnover of ₹80,00,000. Because she qualifies for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD, she declares income as a percentage of turnover rather than computing every expense. She files ITR-4 (Sugam).

Why 6% and 8%?

Under 44AD the deemed profit is 8% of turnover, but only 6% for receipts that came through a bank or digital payment. Going cashless literally lowers her tax.

She does not need

A tax audit, or detailed books of accounts — as long as her declared income stays at or above the prescribed percentage and turnover is within the scheme limit.

9 The big choice

Old regime vs new regime — in one glance

The new regime has lower rates but few deductions. The old regime has higher rates but rewards you for saving and borrowing. Which wins depends entirely on how many deductions you can genuinely claim.

Old regime
New regime (default)
  • Basic exemption up to ₹2,50,000
  • Standard deduction ₹50,000
  • Claim 80C, 80D, HRA, home-loan interest, NPS and more
  • Rebate of ₹12,500 up to ₹5,00,000 income
  • Better only if your deductions are large
  • Basic exemption up to ₹4,00,000
  • Standard deduction ₹75,000
  • Almost no deductions allowed
  • Rebate up to ₹60,000 makes income up to ₹12,00,000 tax-free
  • Usually better for salaried and small earners
A quick rule of thumb

If your total deductions (80C, HRA, home-loan interest, etc.) are small or none, the new regime almost always wins. If you claim a big HRA plus home-loan interest plus the full 80C, run both numbers — the old regime can still come out ahead.

In plain language: what is AIS?

The Annual Information Statement shows financial information reported to the tax department. It can include salary, interest and other transactions. Treat it as a check — not a substitute for your own records.

Do not force a match

Never ignore a mismatch, or change figures just to make them agree. If it stays unclear, get advice from a qualified tax professional before you file.

11 Glossary

Clear up the words before the work.

The handful of terms that trip people up, in one sentence each.

Financial year vs assessment year

The financial year is when you earn the income. The assessment year follows it, and is the year that income is assessed.

AIS, TIS & Form 26AS

AIS shows financial information reported about you; TIS summarises it. Form 26AS helps you check your tax credits. Compare all three with your own records.

Your form & tax regime

Your income and circumstances affect which form and options are available. Check the rules for the applicable assessment year.

TDS & standard deduction

TDS is tax already deducted at source, by your employer or bank. The standard deduction is a flat amount subtracted from salary income before tax.

Presumptive taxation

A scheme that lets small businesses and professionals declare income as a fixed percentage of turnover, instead of maintaining detailed books.

E-verification

Confirming your submitted return with an Aadhaar OTP, net-banking or EVC. Without it, the return is treated as not filed.

12 You are not alone

Know when a second pair of eyes helps.

A guide can explain the journey. A qualified tax professional can help with the decisions specific to you.

  • Multiple income sources, capital gains, or foreign assets.
  • Business books, audit questions, or a notice from the department.
  • Unresolved mismatches, or uncertainty about a claim.
How to prepare for a tax consultation

Bring your PAN and Aadhaar, your Form 16 or business records, your AIS and Form 26AS downloads, a list of your income sources, and any notices you have received. Note your open questions in advance.

14 Avoid these

Ten mistakes that trigger notices

None of these are exotic. They are the ordinary slips that turn a five-minute filing into a months-long correspondence.

  1. Forgetting to e-verify. The return is treated as never filed. Verify within 30 days, every time.
  2. Leaving out bank interest. Banks report it; the department sees it. Declare every rupee of savings and deposit interest.
  3. Mismatched TDS. If your Form 16 and 26AS disagree, fix it with the deductor before filing, not after.
  4. Wrong bank account. An invalid or un-prevalidated account stalls your refund. Pre-validate it in your profile.
  5. Choosing the wrong ITR form. A form that does not match your income sources is rejected outright.
  6. Confusing the years. Filing for the wrong assessment year invalidates the whole exercise.
  7. Ignoring AIS. The Annual Information Statement lists your big transactions. Review it, and dispute anything wrong.
  8. Claiming deductions under the new regime. 80C and HRA simply are not allowed there — claiming them invites scrutiny.
  9. Missing the deadline. Late filing costs a fee, interest, and the ability to carry forward losses.
  10. Not keeping the acknowledgement. Without your ITR-V reference, you cannot prove you filed.

16 Questions

Things people actually ask

Short answers to the doubts that stop most first-timers.

Do I need a Chartered Accountant to file?

No. A simple salaried return, or a small presumptive business return, is designed to be filed by you on the portal. A CA becomes genuinely useful if you have complex capital gains, foreign income, multiple businesses, or you simply prefer the assurance.

What is the difference between AIS and Form 26AS?

Both are the department’s records. 26AS focuses on tax deducted and collected at source. AIS (the Annual Information Statement) is broader — it also lists big transactions like property, shares and interest. Check both before you file.

I changed jobs and have two Form 16s. What now?

Add the salary and TDS from both employers. This is exactly the situation where people under-report and get a notice, so reconcile each Form 16 against 26AS carefully. If a previous employer’s TDS is missing, chase them for it.

How long does a refund take?

Usually a few weeks after your return is processed and e-verified, though it can take longer. You can track it under “Refund Status” on the portal. An unverified return never reaches the refund stage.

What if I made a mistake after filing?

File a revised return — for AY 2026-27 that is generally allowed until 31 March 2027. It replaces your original, so you do not lose your e-verification or your filed status.

Can I file if my income is below the exemption limit?

Yes. You may not be required to, but filing is allowed — and worth doing if tax was deducted from you and you want it back.

Is filing on the government portal really free?

Yes, filing directly on incometax.gov.in costs nothing. You only pay tax, interest or late fees if they apply. Be cautious of websites charging a fee to “submit” on your behalf.

I run a small shop — do I need to keep full books?

If you qualify for presumptive taxation under Section 44AD and declare income at the prescribed percentage, you generally do not need detailed books or a tax audit. Keep clean sales and expense records anyway — you will need the turnover figure.

Can I file my return on this website?

No. Artha Setu is an independent educational guide. You learn and prepare here, then file your return on the official Income Tax e-Filing portal at incometax.gov.in.

What if my AIS does not match my records?

Check the source records first — your bank, employer or broker. If a difference remains, use the AIS feedback facility where appropriate, or speak to a qualified tax professional before filing. Never change figures just to make them agree.

What should I do after submitting?

E-verify within 30 days using an Aadhaar OTP, net-banking or EVC, and save your acknowledgement (ITR-V). Then track processing under “e-Proceedings” and your refund under “Refund Status”.

Do I need to upload my documents here?

No. Your financial documents stay with you. This is a learning space, not a document collection service — nothing you tick or type here is sent anywhere.
